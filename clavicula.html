The Greater Key

大钥匙 · 所罗门之钥

Clavicula Salomonis —— 一部聚焦天使、行星精灵与护符制作的文艺复兴仪式魔法手册。

成书与流传

《所罗门之钥》（Clavicula Salomonis）成书于十四至十五世纪，现存手稿多为意大利文或希腊文，托名所罗门，实为文艺复兴时期仪式魔法的汇编。它与《小钥匙》相比基调更「白」：重心不在召魔，而在召唤天使与行星精灵、净化自身、以及制作护符。十六世纪后它被译为拉丁文、法文、英文，广泛流传，成为西方仪式魔法的经典范本。

The Key of Solomon (Clavicula Salomonis) was compiled in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries; its surviving manuscripts are largely Italian or Greek. Written under Solomon's name, it is in truth a Renaissance anthology of ceremonial magic. Lighter in tone than the Lesser Key, it centres on summoning angels and planetary spirits, self-purification and the making of talismans. After the sixteenth century it spread widely in Latin, French and English translations.

行星魔法

全书以七曜——日、月、金、木、水、火、土——为骨架。每一颗行星都对应特定的时辰、熏香、金属、颜色与符文；施术者须在对应行星的「行星时」行事。这套体系源自新柏拉图主义与占星术：天上星辰的运行被认为与地上万物相应，而魔法正是借由「对应」来调动这些力量。

The work is organised around the seven planets — Sun, Moon, Venus, Jupiter, Mercury, Mars and Saturn. Each planet carries its own hour, incense, metal, colour and characters, and the operator must act within the planet's appointed "planetary hour." The system descends from Neoplatonism and astrology: the motions of the heavens answer to earthly things, and magic works by drawing on those correspondences.

五芒星护符

《所罗门之钥》最著名的是它的「五芒星」（Pentacles）——刻于金属或羊皮纸上的圆盘形护符，每一枚对应一颗行星并服务于特定目的：招财、护身、驱邪、求得爱情或智慧。书中详列护符的图形、铭文与制作时的洁净条件。后世流行的「所罗门护符」图像，大多即出于此。

Best known are its "pentacles" — disc-shaped talismans engraved on metal or parchment, each bound to a planet and serving a specific purpose: wealth, protection, exorcism, love or wisdom. The book sets out their figures, inscriptions and the conditions of purity required to make them. Most later images of "Solomonic talismans" derive from here.

净化与祝圣

与大钥匙相伴的是一整套预备仪式：沐浴斋戒、洁净场地、祝圣工具（刀、杖、杯、杖）、划定魔法圆。全书反复强调施术者自身的「纯洁」——这一伦理框架使《所罗门之钥》更接近一套虔诚的宗教实践，而非单纯的巫术。

With the Key goes a whole apparatus of preparation: bathing and fasting, cleansing the place, consecrating the tools — knife, wand, cup, staff — and drawing the magic circle. Throughout, the text insists on the operator's own "purity." That moral frame brings the Key closer to a pious religious practice than to mere sorcery.
